# Application response draft — not submitted

TACIT explores the judgment hidden inside “remove the wash without disturbing the pellet.” During RNA isolation, that instruction depends on orientation history, limited visual evidence, physical access, and when uncertainty should stop an action.

I developed a browser workbench around that one step. It combines a dimensioned physical model, separately generated camera evidence, a particle-based estimate, bounded action planning, and replayable decision records. A decision record preserves the evidence and checks available before an action. Controlled comparisons rerun the same physical scene while changing history access, camera availability or the controller. Exported case packets preserve both arms and their original camera pixels.

The most informative results include the limits. In the current exploratory study, the readiness controller removed more bulk liquid than a fixed procedure with preflight checks, but neither attained the final target. Independent images still expose renderer-dependent errors. The repaired holder passes nominal computational assembly checks; it has not been physically validated or made fabrication-ready.

That combination of wet-lab context, geometric reasoning and explicit uncertainty is why scientific execution data interests me. TACIT is a focused way to ask what information makes an action executable and how that claim can be inspected.
